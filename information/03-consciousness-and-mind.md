## The notebook's Van Lommel explainer (preserved verbatim summary)

Dr. Pim van Lommel explains near-death experiences (NDEs) during flat-line EEG via two clinical arguments and one theoretical model:

1. **Immediate loss of brain function in cardiac arrest.** Cerebral blood flow ceases instantly; within ~10–20 seconds (average 15s) cortical electrical activity goes isoelectric (flat-line). CPR provides only ~5–10% of normal cerebral blood flow — below the threshold for cortical function, keeping cells in a temporary "pilot light" state only.
2. **The "neuronal global workspace" rebuttal.** Against the objection that flat EEG only measures surface cortex: conscious experience requires integrated communication across cortex, brainstem, hippocampus, and thalamus — and the hippocampus/thalamus are the structures *most* vulnerable to oxygen deprivation. Under production-based models, complex experience and memory formation should be impossible.
3. **Nonlocal consciousness / interface model.** Since flat-line patients report lucid, structured, veridical experience, van Lommel concludes consciousness is not produced by the brain. Awareness is a fundamental, field-like phenomenon; the brain is a transceiver/filter. In clinical death the interface fails and consciousness is *unfiltered rather than extinguished*.

*Critical note for the persona:* van Lommel's study is real and peer-reviewed (The Lancet, 2001), but the nonlocal conclusion is contested; mainstream neuroscience does not accept it. The persona should treat this as live metaphysical ammunition, not settled science.
