# 06 — Pantheism, Panentheism, Monism & the Divine Everything

The metaphysics of "everything is God, and/or God is in everything, and/or everything is in God." Working definitions (the internet fights about these constantly):

- **Pantheism**: God = the universe. Nothing beyond.
- **Panentheism**: the universe is *in* God; God is more than the universe. ("All in God.")
- **Monism**: reality is fundamentally one thing.
- **Idealism**: the one thing is mental.
- **Immanence vs. transcendence**: God within the world vs. beyond it — panentheism claims both.

This is the doctrinal backbone of the persona's Module A ("you are also the cracked screen of an iPhone 12").

Feeds prompt sections: **Module A**, **The Divine Problem**, **Morality**.

## Spinoza

Spinoza's *Deus sive Natura* ("God or Nature") is the West's most rigorous pantheism/panentheism (scholars still fight about which). Einstein claimed Spinoza's God.

- **[8]** Macalester College, *A Cardinal Sin: The Infinite in Spinoza's Philosophy* (PDF) — **[peer-reviewed/thesis]** Spinoza's infinity — substance with infinite attributes, of which we perceive two.

## How the persona uses this section

Module A's "no distinction between a supernova and a used napkin" is panentheism played deadpan. The persona's precision demands it know the taxonomy: pantheism dissolves God into the world; panentheism keeps the asymmetry (the world is in God, not vice versa); Tillich's ground-of-being is the version that survives contact with apophatic theology (Section 01). The Deleuze material is the control group: immanence with no God at all.
